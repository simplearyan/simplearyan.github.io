## 1. Vector Visualization

When we talk about vectors in $\mathbb{R}^2$, we often visualize them as arrows starting from the origin $(0,0)$.

<Callout type="info" title="Definition">
  A **Vector** $v$ in the plane can be represented as a coordinate pair $(a, b)$, which corresponds to an arrow from the origin to the point $(a, b)$.
  
  $$v = a\hat{i} + b\hat{j}$$
</Callout>

Here is a visual representation of two vectors, $(1, 2)$ and $(-1, -1)$.

## 2. Vector Addition

How do we add vectors? Geometrically, we follow the **Parallelogram Law**.

<Callout type="tip" title="The Parallelogram Law">
  If you have two vectors $v$ and $w$, their sum $v + w$ is the diagonal of the parallelogram formed by $v$ and $w$.
</Callout>

<CaptionedFigure 
  src="/images/demo/vector-addition-abstract.png" 
  alt="Parallelogram Rule" 
  caption="Visualizing vector addition v + w using the parallelogram rule." 
/>

### numerical Example

Let's try precise addition. If we have:
*   $v = (1, 2)$
*   $w = (2, 1)$

Then $v + w = (1+2, 2+1) = (3, 3)$.

<CaptionedFigure 
  src="/images/demo/vector-addition-grid.png" 
  alt="Vector Addition Grid" 
  caption="Figure 1.4: Visualization of vector addition (1,2) + (2,1) = (3,3)." 
/>

<Callout type="warning" title="Common Mistake">
  Remember that vector addition is **component-wise**. You cannot simply add the magnitudes together!
  $$|v + w| \neq |v| + |w|$$ (unless they are collinear)
</Callout>

## 3. Quick Concept Review

We can also use interactive cards to summarize key concepts for the student.
