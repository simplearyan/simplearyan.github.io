<CinematicText variant="comic-verse" as="h2" class="mb-6 mt-12" color="#3b82f6">1. Salary Distributions (Box Plot)</CinematicText>

Descriptive statistics often mislead. "Average salary" can be skewed by one billionaire. A **Box Plot** (or Box-and-Whisker) reveals the true spread: the Median, the Interquartile Range (middle 50%), and the outliers.

**Scenario:** Tech Startup Salaries (in $1000s)

<div class="flex flex-col items-center gap-4 my-8">
    <BoxPlot 
        label="Engineering"
        min={80} q1={110} median={135} q3={160} max={190} 
        outliers={[250]}
        width={500} height={120}
        color="#3b82f6"
    />
    <BoxPlot 
        label="Sales"
        min={40} q1={60} median={90} q3={130} max={180} 
        outliers={[]}
        width={500} height={120}
        color="#10b981"
    />
</div>

**Insight:**
While the "max" salaries are similar, the **Median** engineer earns remarkably more ($135k vs $90k). Also, notice the outlier in Engineering (the dot at $250k) — that's the CTO!

<CinematicText variant="comic-verse" as="h2" class="mb-6 mt-12" color="#eab308">2. Joint Probability (Heatmap)</CinematicText>

In Chapter 3, we discuss **Joint Distributions** ($P(X=x, Y=y)$). This is effectively a 2D grid of probabilities. A **Heatmap** allows us to spot the "hot zones" of correlation instantly.

**Scenario:** Survey of Transport vs Commute Time.
*   rows: Car, Bus, Train
*   cols: &lt;15m, 15-30m, &gt;30m

<div class="flex justify-center my-8">
    <Heatmap 
        width={400} 
        height={300}
        rowLabels={["Car", "Bus", "Train"]}
        colLabels={["<15m", "15-30m", ">30m"]}
        data={[
            [0.2, 0.1, 0.05],
            [0.05, 0.25, 0.1],
            [0.0, 0.05, 0.2]
        ]}
    />
</div>

**Analysis:**
The dark blue cell (0.25) shows that the most common outcome is **Bus + 15-30m**. The empty cell (0.0) shows nobody takes a Train for &lt;15m.

<CinematicText variant="comic-verse" as="h2" class="mb-6 mt-12" color="#ef4444">3. A/B Testing Significance (Z-Test)</CinematicText>

When a business runs an A/B test, they calculate a **Z-Score** to see if the new design is truly better. If the score falls into the "critical region" (the tail), the result is **Statistically Significant**.

**Scenario:**
*   Null Hypothesis: No difference ($Z=0$).
*   Observation: We observed a difference of $2.5$ standard errors ($Z=2.5$).

<div class="flex justify-center my-8">
    <ZTestPlot 
        zScore={2.5} 
        tail="right" 
        width={500} 
        height={250} 
    />
</div>

**Conclusion:**
The shading represents the **P-value** (probability of seeing this result by luck). Since the area is tiny (and $Z > 1.96$), we **Reject the Null Hypothesis**. The new design worked!
