<CinematicText variant="epic" as="h1" class="text-4xl md:text-5xl mb-8 text-center" color="#3b82f6">ASSOCIATION BETWEEN TWO VARIABLES</CinematicText>

Chapter 5 of the sources is titled **"Association between two variables"** and explores how information about one variable can provide insights into another. The chapter is divided into three main sections based on the types of variables being compared: <StrokeUnderline color="#3b82f6">categorical-categorical</StrokeUnderline>, <StrokeUnderline color="#3b82f6">numerical-numerical</StrokeUnderline>, and <StrokeUnderline color="#3b82f6">categorical-numerical</StrokeUnderline>.

---

<CinematicText variant="comic-verse" as="h2" class="mb-6 mt-12" color="#3b82f6">1. Association Between Two Categorical Variables</CinematicText>

To determine if an association exists between two categorical variables, researchers use a **contingency table**.

<Concept title="Criteria for Association">
*   **Not Associated:** The row (or column) relative frequencies are the **same** for all rows (or columns).
*   **Associated:** If these frequencies **differ significantly** across rows or columns.
</Concept>

<CinematicText variant="comic-verse" as="h2" class="mb-6 mt-12" color="#3b82f6">2. Association Between Two Numerical Variables</CinematicText>

The sources define several tools to examine the relationship between two quantitative variables.

1.  **Scatter Plots:** A visual test where pairs of values are displayed as points on a two-dimensional plane.
2.  **Describing Patterns:** Check for **Direction** (up/down), **Curvature** (linear/curve), **Variation** (clustering), and **Outliers**.
3.  **Covariance:** Quantifies linear association strength but units are difficult to interpret.
4.  **Pearson Correlation Coefficient ($r$):** Unitless measure between **-1 and +1**.
5.  **Fitting a Line and $R^2$:** Goodness of fit; closer to 1 signifies a good fit.

<Concept title="Example: Car Age vs Price">
A study of car ages and prices showed that as age increases, price decreases.
*   **Trend:** Negative Linear Association ($r \approx -1$).
</Concept>

<CinematicText variant="comic-verse" as="h2" class="mb-6 mt-12" color="#3b82f6">3. Association Between Categorical and Numerical Variables</CinematicText>

When one variable is numerical and the other is categorical with exactly two categories (dichotomous), the **Point Bi-serial Correlation Coefficient ($r_{pb}$)** is used.

<Concept title="Procedure" variant="minimal">
You group the numerical data based on the two categories (often coded as 0 and 1) and compare their means relative to the overall population standard deviation.
</Concept>

<CinematicText variant="comic-verse" as="h2" class="mb-6 mt-12" color="#3b82f6">Practice Questions</CinematicText>

<Problem number="1" title="Categorical Association">
In a study of 10,000 students, 80% of males passed an exam and 79.9% of females passed. Is there an association between gender and passing?

<Solution>
**No.** Because the row relative frequencies (80% and 79.9%) are effectively the same for both rows, the variables are **not associated**.
</Solution>
</Problem>

<Problem number="2" title="Covariance Units">
If the covariance between weight (kg) and height (m) is calculated, what are its units?

<Solution>
The units are **kg $\times$ m**.
</Solution>
</Problem>

<Problem number="3" title="Numerical Trend">
A scatter plot shows that as the size of a house increases, the price also increases in a straight line. How would you describe this association?

<Solution>
The direction is **upward** (positive) and the curvature is **linear**.
</Solution>
</Problem>

<Problem number="4" title="Correlation Fit">
A dataset has a Pearson correlation coefficient ($r$) of -0.95. What does this tell you about the fit and the relationship?

<Solution>
It indicates a **strong negative linear association**.
The $R^2$ value would be $(-0.95)^2 = 0.9025$, meaning the line is a **good fit**, capturing about 90% of the variance.
</Solution>
</Problem>

<Concept title="The Dance Partners Analogy" variant="gamified">
Think of association as **two people dancing together**.

*   **Categorical association:** Checking if people in red shirts always choose partners in blue shirts.
*   **Numerical association:** Watching how their steps move; if one person takes a step forward and the other consistently takes a step forward too, they have a **positive correlation**.
*   The **Correlation Coefficient ($r$)** is the "**synchronisation score**"—a +1 means they are perfectly in sync, while a 0 means they are stepping on each other's toes.
</Concept>
