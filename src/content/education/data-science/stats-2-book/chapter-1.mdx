## Definitions and Properties

We begin by defining the core terms in probability theory.

<Concept title="The Basics">
1.  **Sample Space ($\Omega$)**: The set of all possible outcomes of an experiment.
2.  **Experiment**: A process that leads to one of several possible observations.
3.  **Outcome**: A single result of an experiment.
4.  **Event**: A subset of the sample space.
</Concept>

Probability is formally defined using **Kolmogorov's axioms**, from which we derive fundamental properties like $P(A^c) = 1 - P(A)$.

## Equally Likely Outcomes

When all outcomes in a finite sample space are equally likely, we have the **Uniform Distribution**.

<ArrowEmphasis>
Calculation reduces to counting:
$$P(E) = \frac{\text{Number of outcomes in } E}{\text{Total number of outcomes in } \Omega}$$
</ArrowEmphasis>

## Conditional Probability and Bayes' Theorem

How does knowledge that event $B$ has occurred alter the probability of $A$?

$$P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$$

This leads to one of the most powerful theorems in statistics:

<Concept title="Bayes' Theorem">
$$P(B|A) = \frac{P(A|B)P(B)}{P(A)}$$
This allows us to reverse conditional probabilities, updating our beliefs ($P(B)$) given new evidence ($A$).
</Concept>

## Independence

Events $A$ and $B$ are **independent** if the occurrence of one does not affect the other:

$$P(A \cap B) = P(A)P(B)$$
